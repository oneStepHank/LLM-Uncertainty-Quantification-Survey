# LLM-Uncertainty-Quantification-Survey

Student(s) name: 김한결

Title: A Survey on Quantifying Epistemic Uncertainty in Large Language Models: Metrics, Mechanisms, and Calibration

Summary
The deployment of Large Language Models (LLMs) in high-stakes domains is fundamentally hindered by their susceptibility to hallucinations and overconfident predictions. To mitigate these issues, establishing robust metrics for quantifying the model's epistemic uncertainty is imperative. This report presents a comprehensive survey of contemporary methodologies designed to measure and evaluate uncertainty in LLMs. The survey systematically categorizes quantification metrics into token-level, sequence-level, and semantic-level approaches. A critical focus will be placed on the transition from syntactic probability evaluations to Semantic Entropy, which accounts for the lexical variations inherent in free-form text generation. Furthermore, this report analyzes the mathematical formulations used to isolate epistemic uncertainty from aleatoric noise within autoregressive architectures. Beyond the measurement techniques, the survey evaluates the standard calibration metrics, such as Expected Calibration Error (ECE) and Brier Score, discussing their limitations when applied to generative language tasks.
