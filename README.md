Title: From Predictive Entropy to Semantic Entropy: A Survey of Uncertainty Metrics for Large Language Models

Summary
Reliable uncertainty estimation is a critical requirement for deploying Large Language Models (LLMs) in real-world applications. Traditional uncertainty metrics primarily rely on token probabilities and predictive entropy, which often fail to capture semantic equivalence in free-form text generation. This survey reviews the evolution of uncertainty quantification metrics in LLMs, focusing on the transition from token-level probability measures to semantic-level approaches such as Semantic Entropy. The survey compares the theoretical foundations, mathematical formulations, and empirical performance of existing uncertainty metrics. Additionally, it discusses the challenges of evaluating uncertainty in open-ended generation tasks and identifies key directions for future research in semantic-aware uncertainty estimation.
